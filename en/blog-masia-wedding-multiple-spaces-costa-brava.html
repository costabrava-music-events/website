Masia wedding with multiple spaces: how to coordinate sound, lighting and music without breaking the flow

Many weddings in Costa Brava follow the same route: outdoor ceremony, garden cocktail hour, dinner in a courtyard or room, and the final party somewhere else. When each part happens in a different area, music and technical production cannot be treated as one single setup.

Each area needs its own setup

The ceremony needs clear speech, carefully timed entrance music and clean coverage. Cocktail hour needs atmosphere without covering conversation. Dinner often needs microphones for speeches, and the party needs a different level of power, booth setup and light.

That is why it helps to separate needs by area and schedule instead of talking about “equipment for the whole wedding” as if everything happened in one place.

The most common mistake is moving everything at the last minute

If the same table, the same pair of speakers or one booth has to cover the ceremony, cocktail hour and dance floor, the risk is obvious: gaps between blocks, rushed changeovers and dead time that cools the room.

When the wedding uses multiple spaces, it usually works better to plan a stable base in each area or a pre-defined transition inside the technical event production.

Sound, microphones and power have to be planned together

It is not enough to know where the DJ will stand. Distances, power points, access, limiters and whether there will be speeches, live music or a guest sax player also matter.

In a masia, lighting does more than decorate. It marks how the wedding moves, keeps continuity when daylight fades and makes dinner and party feel connected even when they happen in separate areas.

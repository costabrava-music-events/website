Product launch in Barcelona: sound, lighting and music that make the brand clear and memorable

A presentation does not begin when the screen turns on. It begins when guests arrive, know where to look and feel that something is about to happen. Sound, light and music are not decoration: they organise attention and give a brand the stage it needs.

In Barcelona, a product launch may sit alongside dinner, cocktails, press, distributors or a sales team. The task is not to add more equipment; it is to decide what each minute needs to do. A restrained welcome invites conversation, a clear cue marks the beginning and an energetic close stops the message from disappearing when guests start talking again.

Start with the guest journey

Before choosing speakers or fixtures, map the actual journey: arrival, registration, product area, presentation, questions, catering and departure. Each zone has a job. If welcome music spills into the demo area, or the screen is backlit, people lose information even when the setup looks polished.

A simple running order prevents this: doors open, arrival music, five-minute warning, presenter walk-on, video, demo, questions and move into cocktails. Sharing it with the agency, venue, speakers and technical team lets everyone prepare transitions instead of solving them in front of guests.

Make the voice the technical priority

At a launch, a brand message competes with the room acoustics, air conditioning and arrival noise. Choose microphones around who is speaking and how they move: a headset works for a dynamic demo, a handheld can suit interviews and Q&A, and a lectern needs a height and distance check before doors open.

The system should cover the listening area evenly, without making the front row uncomfortably loud. In a long room or one with columns, split coverage is often more effective than putting everything beside the stage. For speeches and audiovisual content, event sound hire should include voices, video playback and someone to manage live changes.

Use light to guide attention

Lighting creates hierarchy: a welcoming arrival, a visible product, a well-lit presenter and a readable screen. Coloured light can dress a wall or photo area, but it should not distort a garment, package or detail guests need to see accurately. Check reflections on screens, glossy materials and glass before event day.

A single lighting shift at the product reveal creates a recognisable moment without turning the launch into a show disconnected from the brand. Afterwards, warmer light for cocktails helps conversations continue and keeps event photography coherent.

Give music a clear job

On arrival, music should leave room for conversation. Just before the show, it can raise expectation. During a demo or video, it must leave space for content. At the end, it can lead into networking, dinner or celebration. You do not need an endless playlist; you need a selection that fits the brand tone and the guests in the room.

If the format ends with dinner or a party, bringing music for private events or a DJ into the briefing prevents an abrupt handover. The same team can move between microphones, video and music without breaking the atmosphere.

Rehearse signals and appoint a coordinator

A rehearsal is not only a microphone check. It is where timings are measured, video versions are confirmed, presenter entrances are tried and clear signals are agreed. Someone needs to know who calls the video, when the music fades and what happens if a section runs long.
